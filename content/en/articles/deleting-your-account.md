Cancelling stops the money. Deleting removes the account. People do the first,
assume it did the second, and leave years of conversation on a server belonging
to a company they have stopped paying attention to.

## Do it in this order

**1. Export, if an export exists.** Once the account is gone, nothing can be
recovered. If the app offers a data download, run it first and check the file
actually contains the conversations rather than a summary of your billing.

**2. Remove payment details**, where the app allows it independently.

**3. Cancel the subscription** and read the confirmation. Note whether access
continues to the end of the period.

**4. Delete the account** — a separate action, usually in a different settings
screen, sometimes only by email.

**5. Verify.** Try to log in a week later. An account that still accepts your
password was not deleted.

Skipping step one is irreversible. Skipping step five is how people discover, a
year later, that the request never completed.

## What typically survives

Even an honoured deletion rarely removes everything at once:

- **Backups** roll off on their own schedule, commonly 30–90 days.
- **Billing records** are retained because tax law requires it. This is normal
  and not negotiable.
- **Moderation records** of flagged content are often kept longer.
- **Generated images** sometimes live in separate storage from the chat and are
  not always covered by the same request.
- **Aggregated or anonymised data** derived from your usage usually stays, and
  is generally outside the scope of a deletion right.

The reasonable expectation is that your conversations and profile disappear
promptly, and that a documented tail exists for the rest. The unreasonable one
is that a company with a legal retention duty will ignore it for you.

## If the app makes it hard

Self-service deletion is the norm among apps worth using. When it is missing,
you still have leverage:

In the EU and UK, a deletion request under GDPR must be answered within one
month. In California and a growing number of US states, comparable rights apply.
Write to the privacy contact in the policy, state plainly that this is a
deletion request under the relevant law, and keep the email.

## The part people do not expect

Deleting a companion you used daily for months is not a neutral administrative
act for everyone, and there is no reason to pretend otherwise. If it feels like
something, that is a normal response to months of routine, not a sign that
anything is wrong with you.

Exporting the conversation first makes the decision reversible in the only sense
that matters — you keep what was said, without leaving it on someone else's
server. [Replika](/reviews/replika-review/), being the app most likely to have
accumulated years of history, is where this comes up most often.
