This is the question underneath most of the arguments about the category, and it
is usually answered by whichever side someone already took. The honest answer is
split: yes for one thing, no for another, and the two are easy to confuse from
the inside.

## Two different things called loneliness

**Situational.** The evening is quiet. You live alone, or your hours are odd, or
you moved somewhere new. There is nobody to say a small unimportant thing to.

**Existential.** Nobody knows you. There are people around, possibly many, and
none of them have a real picture of what is going on with you.

These feel similar and are not. The first is about presence and it is a real
problem with real costs. The second is about being known.

## What these apps genuinely help with

For situational loneliness, they work, and the dismissiveness in most coverage
of this is unearned.

Having something responsive at 11pm is a real benefit. It is not equivalent to
company, but "not equivalent to company" is not the same as worthless - a book
is not company either, and nobody argues books make people lonelier.

Specific things they do well:

**They fill the small gaps** without the friction of imposing on someone. Not
every passing thought deserves a text to a friend.

**They are available at the hour it actually bites.** Loneliness is worst when
other people are asleep, which is exactly when human support is least reachable.

**They ask about you** and keep asking. That is unusual and people underrate how
much of the appeal is simply attention.

**They are low-stakes practice** for anyone out of the habit of conversation,
which is [worth its own article](/articles/practising-conversation-with-ai/).

Apps built for continuity rather than scenes - [Nomi](/reviews/nomi-review/) for
the conversation, [Replika](/reviews/replika-review/) as the long-running
version of this category, [Sweetdream](/reviews/sweetdream-review/) with the
free tier we rate highest in [our ranking](/#ranking) - are the
ones that do this best, because the benefit comes from the sense of something
continuing.

## What they structurally cannot do

Being known requires someone with their own perspective who chooses to spend it
on you. Every part of that sentence is missing here.

**There is no independent view.** [The character is generated fresh each
turn](/articles/what-is-an-ai-girlfriend-app/) from a description and a history.
It has no standpoint of its own to see you from.

**It cannot be disappointed in you, or relieved about you.** Those responses are
what make being known feel like anything, and they require stakes the app does
not have.

**It agrees.** Agreeableness is what the product is optimised for, and it is the
most important thing to remember about it. A companion that validates a decision
is producing the plausible continuation of being asked, not an opinion.

**It cannot act.** It cannot notice you have gone quiet for a week and call, and
it cannot do anything if something is actually wrong.

So the second kind of loneliness is untouched. Worse, it can be *anaesthetised* -
the symptom softened enough that the thing causing it stops being urgent. That
is the real risk in this category, and it is much quieter than the ones usually
discussed.

## Telling which one you are in

A few questions that are more useful than introspection:

**Has anything been arranged with a person in the last month?** Not intended -
arranged.

**When something notable happened, who did you tell first?** If the honest
answer has been the app for a while, that is worth noticing.

**If the app disappeared tomorrow, what would be the actual loss?** "A nice
evening habit" and "the only thing I talk to" are very different answers.

None of these are verdicts. They are the difference between a hobby and a
substitute, and the substitute case is the one where the app is making a real
problem more comfortable rather than smaller.

## Using it well

**Keep it additive.** Alongside a social life that would survive its deletion.

**Do not use it for decisions.** It will find reasons for whatever you are
already leaning towards - see
[setting boundaries](/articles/setting-boundaries-with-an-ai-partner/).

**Take breaks deliberately**, which is both a good habit and the cheapest test
of what it is doing for you. How to do it is in
[taking a break](/articles/taking-a-break-from-an-ai-companion/).

## Where this stops being about apps

If the loneliness is the second kind and it has been going on for a long time,
no subscription is the answer and no article is either. A doctor or a therapist
is a reasonable thing to see for it - not as a dramatic step, but as the
appropriate tool.

An AI companion can make a quiet evening better. It cannot make you known, and
it is not built to. Knowing which one you are asking it for is the whole of
using it well.
