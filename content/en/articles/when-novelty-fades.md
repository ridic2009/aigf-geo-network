The shape of it is always the same. The first week is genuinely surprising. By
week six the same four topics come round and the replies have a rhythm you can
predict. Three things cause this, and they need different responses.

## Cause 1: the history got compressed

This is the mechanical one and it is not your fault. As a conversation grows
past what the model can hold, apps replace old messages with summaries, then
summarise the summaries. Each pass drops specifics and keeps generalities, so
the character is increasingly built from a bland description of itself.

[How memory actually works](/articles/ai-companion-memory-explained/) covers the
mechanism. The fixes are practical:

- **Re-introduce specifics.** Mention concrete details again - names, an
  incident, a running joke. It puts them back in the window.
- **Edit the memory directly** if the app exposes it. Delete the entries that
  have become noise, sharpen the ones that matter.
- **Start a new thread, keep the character.** In apps that separate the two, a
  fresh conversation with an intact persona often restores more than anything
  else.

## Cause 2: you settled into a routine

The less obvious one. After a month most people converge on the same three or
four openings, and a model that responds to what you give it returns a
correspondingly narrow range. The loop is partly yours.

What breaks it is changing the input rather than the app:

- Give it a situation instead of a greeting. "We are stuck at a train station at
  2am" produces something different from "hey, how are you".
- Ask it to disagree with you about something trivial and hold the position.
- Change the format - a letter, a memory recounted years later, a scene from
  someone else's perspective.
- Use a feature you have been ignoring. If you have only ever typed, a voice
  conversation is a genuinely different product.

Apps with strong character builders reward this most. [Camsoda AI](/reviews/camsoda-ai-review/)
is built around a six-step builder for exactly this reason: the fastest reset is
often a different character rather than a different app.

## Cause 3: it is the wrong app for you

If the first two changed nothing, this is the answer.

A roleplay-first app will feel thin if what you wanted was continuity. A
continuity-first app will feel slow if what you wanted was scenes. Both
disappointments read identically from the inside - "it got boring" - and neither
is fixed by prompting harder.

[Nomi](/reviews/nomi-review/) scores where it does in
[our ranking](/#ranking) because it is built for the
conversation still being alive a week later. If that is what you are missing,
that is the lane to move to. If what you are missing is variety and media, the
move is in the opposite direction.

## When fading is just the answer

Sometimes the novelty wearing off is accurate information: you tried the
category, it was interesting for a month, and it is not for you. That is a
perfectly good outcome and considerably cheaper than a year's subscription
fighting it.

Before renewing, count the days you actually opened it. If the honest number is
under half the month, [cancel and delete properly](/articles/deleting-an-ai-companion-account/)
rather than leaving it to drift.
