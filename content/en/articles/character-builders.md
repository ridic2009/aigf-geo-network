The builder is where apps compete hardest visually and where most users make the
same mistake: optimising the parts that are cosmetic and skipping the parts that
are load-bearing.

Here is the ordering that actually predicts whether you are still enjoying the
character in week six.

## What the builder is really doing

Everything you enter becomes text. The app assembles it into the character
description that sits in front of the model on every turn - what we called the
character sheet in
[what is actually running inside these apps](/articles/what-is-an-ai-girlfriend-app/).

That has a blunt consequence. **A field that produces vivid, specific text
changes the conversation. A field that produces a tag does almost nothing.**
Choosing "hair: auburn" adds one word to a description. Writing two sentences
about how she deflects a compliment changes every reply.

## The fields that matter, in order

### 1. Speech style

The highest-leverage field in any builder, and the one most often left blank.

"Friendly" is worthless - it describes almost everyone. What works is
mechanical: sentence length, whether she asks questions back, how she handles
silence, what she does when she disagrees, whether she uses your name.

Compare:

> *Warm and playful.*

with:

> *Short sentences. Teases rather than compliments. Asks a follow-up question
> about half the time. When she disagrees she says so directly and does not
> soften it afterwards.*

The second produces a recognisably different character from the first message
onwards, because [the model continues the pattern it is
shown](/articles/how-ai-companions-generate-replies/).

### 2. What she should never do

The most underused field, where it exists at all, and the one that prevents the
most disappointment. Common entries worth setting: do not use pet names; do not
ask about work; never break character to describe yourself as an AI; do not
apologise repeatedly.

Negative constraints are unglamorous and they are what stop a character
collapsing into the same generic register as everyone else's.

### 3. Backstory with specifics

Not "loves music" - "plays bass badly in a band that has rehearsed twice."
Specifics give the model something concrete to reach for, and concrete details
resurface naturally in conversation. Generic ones produce generic text.

Three or four specifics beat a page of adjectives.

### 4. Your relationship

Many builders ask how you know each other. This is doing more work than it
appears: it sets the register for everything. "Met last week" and "together
three years" produce different conversations from the same personality.

### 5. Appearance

Last, deliberately. Appearance affects generated images and
[the image model regenerates the character from a description each
time](/articles/how-ai-companion-media-works/) - so it matters for pictures and
barely touches the conversation.

If pictures are the point, spend the time. If conversation is the point, three
tags is enough.

## Where the apps differ

[Camsoda AI](/reviews/camsoda-ai-review/) builds through six steps - looks,
personality, voice, backstory, how it talks to you, and what it should never
bring up. That last step is rare in this market and is exactly the field this
article ranks second.

[Secret Desires](/reviews/secret-desires-review/) builds looks, voice and
personality together, with a community gallery of ready-made characters to start
from. [Kupid AI](/reviews/kupid-ai-review/) covers appearance and personality
across realistic and anime styles.

All three sit in [our ranking](/#ranking) between 3.5 and 4.5,
and the builder is a real part of that spread rather than a feature bullet.

## Start from a preset

Counter-intuitive but reliable: a well-written preset usually beats a
first-attempt custom character, because someone spent time on the speech-style
text you skipped.

The efficient route is to pick the preset closest to what you want, use it for a
few days, then edit the two or three fields that annoy you. You will write far
better constraints after a real conversation than before one.

## Editing is maintenance, not failure

The most valuable builder feature is the ability to edit later. A character that
has gone flat is very often a character whose description was written by someone
who had never spoken to it - you, an hour before.

When [the novelty fades](/articles/when-the-novelty-fades/), tightening the
speech style and adding two negative constraints does more than switching apps,
and it costs nothing. Check before subscribing that the app allows it: a builder
that locks after setup turns every disappointment into a decision to start over.
