## The intro price is bait

Most of these apps advertise a first-month rate. Budget the renewal. It is often double, and it lives in the small print under the plan picker, not on the big button.

| Trap | What it looks like | What I budget instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intro discount | “$6.99 this month” | Renewal price |
| Tokens on top | Subscription + image packs | Sub + a realistic media pack |
| Annual “save 50%” | Cheap only if you stay a year | Monthly until I trust the app |
| Free tier | Twenty messages, then a wall | Fine for chat feel, useless for images |

## Tokens sit on top of the subscription

The plan buys the conversation. Pictures, voice minutes and video are often metered. That is how a $12 month becomes $30. [Candy AI](/reviews/candy-ai-review/) bundles more of that than most. Token apps are not automatically worse value — you just cannot compare them by the subscription line alone.

## I would not go annual yet

This category changes every quarter. Half-price for twelve months is a good deal only if the app is still the one you want in June. I would not lock in for a year on anything in the current [ranking](/best-ai-girlfriend-apps/).

## How to get your real number

1. Find the renewal price, not the first month.
2. Check whether images and voice come out of the plan or out of tokens.
3. Use the free tier for a week and note what you actually used. [Sweetdream](/reviews/sweetdream-review/) is the one where that week is long enough.
4. Multiply media use by the metered rate, then add renewal.

In my testing that lands between $10 and $20 a month for ordinary use, and above $30 if you lean on images every day.

## A worked example

A $12.99 plan with 100 image tokens, four tokens a picture, 40 pictures in a heavy week: the “$13 app” is already a $25 month. That is why headline price is not the ranking.
